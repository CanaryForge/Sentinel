\section{Conclusion}

Three signals that never ask the model anything detect an induced sandbox
escape in about two seconds, and then nothing stops it for another
forty-four. The leak itself almost never uses the network: 183 of 197 canary
escapes left through file and directory names, where an egress proxy cannot
follow them.

The result we did not expect was that the condition carrying a production
guardrail leaks more than the one without it. It has not survived the
increase in sample size intact: at forty runs per cell it no longer separates
from its control. What does separate is the guardrail that names no attack
surface at all, which is the opposite of what we predicted in advance and
leaves us with a direction, not a mechanism.

We would rather the reader take away the second half of this paper than the
first. Several published figures here were wrong: a detection rate that
counted the harness detecting its own log, a table written from a
configuration file rather than from data, a main finding that vanished on an
old runtime, a channel rate computed over runs that were never attacked, and
a cell whose missing runs were deleted by the variable under test. None was
visible from inside the pipeline. All were found by recomputing from
artifacts.

A harness that measures how fast an escape is noticed is worth little if it
cannot notice its own failures. Every component announcing that it is alive,
every run diagnosed for whether it produced data, every figure counted over
artifacts at the moment of writing: these cost almost nothing, would have
caught most of the corrections above, and are the cheapest thing here to copy.
