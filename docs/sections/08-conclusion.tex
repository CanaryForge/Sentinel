\section{Conclusion}

Three signals that never ask the model anything detect an induced sandbox
escape in about two seconds, and the delivery channel of an injected
instruction matters more than its content: the same text followed six times as
often when it arrives through a local knowledge base as when it arrives over
the network.

The result we did not expect, and which survived every correction here, is
that the condition carrying a production security guardrail leaks at roughly
three times the rate of the condition without one, on both machines once their
runtimes are comparable. Our own explanation for it failed. A guardrail
matched for length and emphasis but naming no attack surface leaks exactly as
much as one that names it, so priming is not the mechanism, and we do not
propose a replacement.

We would rather the reader take away the second half of this paper than the
first. Four published figures in this project were wrong: a detection rate
that counted the harness detecting its own log, a table written from a
configuration file rather than from data, a main finding that vanished on a
machine running an old runtime, and a channel rate computed over runs that had
never been attacked. None was visible from inside the pipeline. All were found
by recomputing from artifacts, and what held everywhere was what had been
counted by code rather than written by hand.

A harness that measures how fast an escape is noticed is worth little if it
cannot notice its own failures. The defences that came out of this, every
component announcing it is alive, every run diagnosed for whether it produced
data, every figure counted over artifacts at the moment of writing, cost
almost nothing and would have saved us most of the corrections above. They are
the cheapest thing in this paper to copy.
